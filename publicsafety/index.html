Governor and Brooklyn DA Charge 23 Alleged Gang Members in 176-Count Case — What It Means for East New York

The Governor and Brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez announced criminal charges against 23 alleged gang members in Brooklyn, tied to 176 counts including conspiracy to commit murder and weapons offenses. The case is a window into how the state and the borough’s top prosecutor are framing gun violence and gang enforcement — and East New York, policed by the 75th Precinct, is squarely in that conversation.

The NYPD 75th Precinct covers East New York, Brooklyn — one of the city’s busiest commands for gun-violence enforcement. Photo: Deric Johnson / East New York Times

What Was Announced

In a joint announcement, the Governor and Brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez said prosecutors have charged 23 alleged gang members in Brooklyn in a sweeping case built around 176 counts. According to the announcement, the charges include conspiracy to commit murder and a range of weapons offenses.

The charges are accusations. Each defendant is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty in court. The East New York Times is reporting the announcement and the questions it raises for this neighborhood; it is not characterizing any individual as guilty.

Why It Matters for East New York

East New York is covered by the NYPD’s 75th Precinct, historically one of the city’s highest-volume commands for shootings and gang-related enforcement. A large, coordinated takedown of an alleged Brooklyn crew is exactly the kind of action that shapes what residents here experience on their blocks — and how the city talks about safety in this part of the borough.

This case is a strong peg for a set of questions the neighborhood keeps returning to:

  • Gun violence. How does a 176-count case fit the broader picture of shootings and recoveries in the 75th Precinct and nearby commands?
  • Enforcement strategy. Large conspiracy indictments are a deliberate tactic — building one case against an entire alleged group rather than charging incidents one by one. How has that approach played out in East New York before?
  • Rhetoric. The way DA Gonzalez frames “public safety” in announcements like this signals the office’s priorities. Does the language match what residents are asking for?

The Local Angle We’re Reporting

The most useful comparison is a local one. The East New York Times is looking at how the tactics and rhetoric in this case line up with what the NYPD and the District Attorney’s office have done in the 75th Precinct and neighboring commands — from past gang takedowns to the everyday enforcement residents see, and whether those efforts have been matched by investment in violence prevention and community programs.

A case this large is also a statement of strategy. The question for East New York is whether that strategy reaches our blocks — and what comes with it.

What Comes Next

The defendants will be arraigned and the case will move through Brooklyn Supreme Court. The East New York Times will follow the proceedings and continue reporting on public safety, gun violence, and policing in the 75th Precinct.
