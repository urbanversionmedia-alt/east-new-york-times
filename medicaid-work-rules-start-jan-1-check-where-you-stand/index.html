Medicaid Work Rules Start Jan. 1. Here's How to Check Where You Stand.

A free city tool walks you through the new SNAP and Medicaid rules one question at a time. If you're exempt, you may still have to prove it.

By Deric JohnsonThe East New York TimesNovember 19, 20264 min read

Update, Nov. 19, 2026: New York will use the federal hold harmless period, so members won't lose coverage for failing the work rules before July 1, 2027. Read the update below.

In June, The East New York Times explained what the new federal Medicaid work rules could mean for our neighborhood (Part 1 and Part 2). The rules start Jan. 1, 2027. That's six weeks from now. The city estimates about 900,000 New Yorkers could lose their health coverage under the new rules (Mayor's Office, Oct. 27, 2026). Here's what you need to know and who to call.

Who has to follow the rules

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, some Medicaid members ages 19 to 64 will have to show they work, go to school, train for a job or volunteer to keep their coverage (NYC Health Department, checked Nov. 19, 2026).

To meet the rule in a month, you need to do one of three things, according to NY State of Health (Aug. 10, 2026). You can earn at least $580. You can spend at least 80 hours working, going to school at least half time, in job training or volunteering. Or you can mix some earnings with some hours to reach the same total.

The state checks when you apply or renew on or after Jan. 1, 2027 (NY State of Health, Aug. 10, 2026). If you already have Medicaid, the rules could apply starting with March 2027 renewals (NYC Health Department, checked Nov. 19, 2026). Most adults 19 to 64 will also have to renew every six months instead of once a year (NY State of Health, Aug. 10, 2026).

If the state can't verify that you meet the rule, it has to send you a notice and give you 30 days to show you meet it or that it doesn't apply to you. If you don't respond, your application could be denied or your coverage could end (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, June 1, 2026).

Who is exempt

You don't have to meet the work rule if you are any of the following, according to NY State of Health (Aug. 10, 2026):

  • 65 or older, or entitled to or enrolled in Medicare Part A, or enrolled in Part B
  • Pregnant, or were pregnant in the last 12 months
  • A parent, guardian, caretaker relative or family caregiver of a child under 14 or of a person with a disability
  • Enrolled in Medicaid with a disability
  • Diagnosed with a physical or mental health condition that makes it hard to work
  • In a program for drug or alcohol use
  • In a SNAP household and subject to SNAP work rules, or enrolled in TANF and meeting its work rules
  • In jail or prison, or released in the last three months
  • In foster care, or under 26 and aged out of foster care
  • A veteran with a total disability
  • American Indian or Alaska Native

Being exempt doesn't always mean you're done. You may have to show proof when you renew (NY State of Health, Aug. 10, 2026). In 2027, people can self-report some exemptions, including the "medically frail" exemption. That option will be restricted in 2028 and after, according to a July 27, 2026 letter from the NYC Health Department to health care providers.

How to use the city's checker

The city launched nyc.gov/workrules on Oct. 27 (Mayor's Office, Oct. 27, 2026). Choose your language at the top of the page. It's available in English, Spanish, Haitian Creole, Bengali, Chinese, Arabic, Urdu, French, Korean, Polish and Russian (nyc.gov/workrules, checked Nov. 19, 2026).

The first question asks which benefits you get or are applying for: SNAP, Medicaid or both. From there, you answer questions about your age, work, school and other circumstances. At the end, you'll see whether the rules may apply to you, whether you may be exempt, or what else you need to do (Mayor's Office, Oct. 27, 2026).

The checker is a guide. It does not give you an official decision (nyc.gov/workrules, checked Nov. 19, 2026).

What to do now

Update your phone number, email and address with NY State of Health so you don't miss a notice (NYC Health Department, checked Nov. 19, 2026).

Open every letter and email from NY State of Health. Members who may have to follow the new rules started getting notices by mail, or by email if they chose that, from mid-August through Sept. 30, 2026 (NYS Medicaid Update, September 2026).

If your Medicaid is tied to Cash Assistance, the state is separating the two starting Jan. 1, 2027. HRA says those households will get a text from NY State of Health and a mailed letter with instructions in December. Log in to ACCESS HRA and make sure your mailing address and phone number are right (HRA, checked Nov. 19, 2026).

Who to call

  • NYC Health Department counselors: 866-INSURED (866-467-8733). Free and available in many languages (NYC Health Department, checked Nov. 19, 2026).
  • NY State of Health: 855-355-5777, TTY 800-662-1220. Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., Saturday 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. (NY State of Health, checked Nov. 19, 2026).
  • HRA Medicaid Helpline: 888-692-6116, for people whose Medicaid is handled by HRA, including people 65 and older and people with disabilities (HRA, checked Nov. 19, 2026).
  • HRA SNAP hotline: 718-762-7669, Monday to Friday 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. (nyc.gov/workrules, checked Nov. 19, 2026).
  • NYC Health + Hospitals financial counselors: 1-844-NYC-4NYC (1-844-692-4692), then press 3 (nyc.gov/workrules, checked Nov. 19, 2026).
  • If you can't get insurance or can't afford it: NYC Care offers affordable care at NYC Health + Hospitals regardless of income or immigration status. Call 646-NYC-CARE (646-692-2273) or 311 (nyc.gov/workrules and NYC Health Department, checked Nov. 19, 2026).

Update: New York will pause coverage losses until July

Updated Nov. 19, 2026. New York will use the federal hold harmless period. Gov. Kathy Hochul announced Thursday that the state will opt in through June 30, 2027. During that time, Medicaid members will not lose coverage for failing to meet the new work rules. The rules themselves still begin Jan. 1, 2027 (Governor's Office, Nov. 19, 2026).

The state says it will use the extra six months to test its systems, reach members and fix problems before the pause ends (Governor's Office, Nov. 19, 2026).

The governor also directed the state Health Department to ask federal officials to exempt all Bronx residents from the work rules because of high unemployment. The Bronx is the only county in New York that meets that standard right now. The state says it will keep watching unemployment in every county and seek more exemptions where they qualify (Governor's Office, Nov. 19, 2026).

The state is also adding free help. It is putting $32 million a year into 49 community groups that help people sign up for coverage, fill out paperwork and understand their benefits, with more awards planned for early 2027 (Governor's Office, Nov. 19, 2026).

The announcement does not say whether members will still get work rule notices during the pause, or whether they should answer them. The state Health Department told The East New York Times it is working on answers to our questions. Until then, the state's advice hasn't changed: keep your mailing address, phone number and email current, and read every notice from NY State of Health (Governor's Office, Nov. 19, 2026).
