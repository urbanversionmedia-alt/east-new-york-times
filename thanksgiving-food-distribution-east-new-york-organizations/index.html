Thanksgiving Food Distribution Includes East New York Organizations

Reynoso says the annual Borough Hall effort brings food closer to families through community organizations.

By Deric JohnsonThe East New York TimesNovember 21, 20263 min read

Organizations serving East New York were among the groups participating in a Thanksgiving food distribution outside Brooklyn Borough Hall, where Borough President Antonio Reynoso said the goal was to bring assistance closer to families across the borough.

Reynoso said 150 organizations were participating, including groups in Community District 5. He described the approach as a way to reach residents who otherwise would have to travel to Downtown Brooklyn for food.

“We don’t want folks to have to travel one hour to get food,” Reynoso told The East New York Times. “We want to make sure that the community really feels that the resources they need get to them.”

Reaching East New York

During the interview, a staff member identified Crystal Church and a Bangladeshi organization as participating groups in Community District 5.

Reynoso said distributing food through organizations throughout Brooklyn was intended to make the effort more equitable.

“Traditionally when we did this in the past, only the people that can make it to Borough Hall really benefited from it,” he said.

He said it was unfair to require residents to travel to Downtown Brooklyn and emphasized that participating organizations were located throughout the borough.

Reynoso also pointed to grocery costs as a concern for families preparing for Thanksgiving.

“We know that groceries have gotten so expensive and there are many families that might not be able to have a complete dinner for Thanksgiving,” he said.

He said the goal was to make families’ lives easier and help them have a more joyous holiday.

Providing the Sides

Deputy Brooklyn Borough President the Rev. Kim Council said the annual distribution brought together the borough president’s office and corporate sponsors to provide food to community-based organizations.

Council described it as a longstanding Brooklyn tradition that preceded the current administration. She said corporate sponsors help provide resources to purchase food for distribution throughout the borough.

Council said the distribution complemented turkey giveaways offered by local elected officials.

“I think your local electeds will give you a whole bunch of turkeys, but we’re gonna provide the sides,” she said.

Bringing Resources to the Community

Reynoso said the effort had grown over the years, with more food and more organizations participating.

For East New York, he emphasized making resources accessible within the community rather than requiring residents to travel across Brooklyn.

“East New York, keep your head up and happy Thanksgiving,” he said.
